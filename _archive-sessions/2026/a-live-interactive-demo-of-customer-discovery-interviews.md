It’s important not to start a business or project that serves other people with assumptions of what they want and need.

Most people are agreeable. If you ask them, “Do you like this tool? Would you buy/use it?”, they will show enthusiasm. It can be painful (and sometimes expensive!) to realize that all those people were just being nice. Even if they had good intentions, they never had the drive to go out of their way to use or buy the software, hardware, or service you worked so hard to develop.

So how do you find out what people will really buy or use? Customer discovery is a semi-structured method for identifying what really motivates potential customers. The goal is to discover what would motivate the customer so strongly that it would be worth abandoning a routine: It could be switching to an open-source tool, hiring you as a freelancer, or buying a product.

The presenter has been coaching a team of local techies interested in starting a worker-owned cooperative in Seattle. The group has recently conducted dozens of customer discovery interviews. Members of this team will play the role of customer in the live demo interviews. Audience members will also have the option to volunteer to be interviewed.

This demo session will start with a hypothesis about a customer need: “If [product/service] solves [problem] then [specific type of customer] will adopt the solution.” The audience will learn how to document and analyze the interview data to support or disprove the hypothesis.

The customer discovery process takes practice, but is not particularly complex, and audience members should leave the session with the knowledge to conduct and analyze their own interviews.
